The Butterfly Effect

The Gifts We Are

Kendall Evans · July 6, 2026

For years, I thought I was searching for my purpose.

I thought purpose was something you eventually found after enough wrong turns, enough heartbreak, enough failure, enough wondering if you were ever going to figure life out.

Looking back now, I don't think I was searching for my gift.

I think my gift had been quietly introducing itself to me my entire life.

I just didn't recognize it.

Growing up, my parents always told me I had a gift working with older adults.

It wasn't just them.

My whole family said it.

They'd tell me I had a way of connecting with people that couldn't really be taught. Somehow people trusted me. They opened up to me. They felt comfortable around me. I never forced conversations. I simply listened, cared, and genuinely wanted to know their stories.

I always brushed it off.

I thought everyone did that.

I didn't realize that what comes naturally to us is often the very thing we're meant to give away.

Ironically, after years of trying to figure out what career I was meant to have, I'm beginning to realize that maybe I wasn't supposed to become someone different.

Maybe I was always meant to become more of who I already was.

And maybe that's why this chapter has two stories.

Purpose.

Loss.

By the end of it all, they tie together seamlessly.

Now I don't think they were ever separate stories at all.

Around the same time my dad's memory began changing, another person unexpectedly entered my life.

His name was Mike.

I met him through one of my closest friends.

Mike and she had been best friends for years.

When Mike could no longer safely care for himself because of dementia, she refused to let him go through it alone. Rather than let him spend that chapter of his life without someone who truly loved him, she opened her home to him.

At the time, I happened to be living there too.

Looking back now, it's almost impossible not to notice how strangely life connected everything.

Years before, she had worked as a nursing assistant in a nursing home.

She had spent her career caring for older adults.

Then she became Mike's caregiver.

Years later…

She's the very same person who's taken me in while I've been rebuilding my own life.

When I lost everything and everyone I ever loved, not only once, but twice.

As I write this, I'm sitting on her couch.

The same home that became Mike's safe place became mine too.

After everything that happened over the past several years, there were times trauma affected me so deeply that I wasn't fully capable of taking care of myself the way I once had. Little by little, I've fought my way back. I've done the work. I've kept going when giving up would've been easier.

Now I feel like I'm standing at the finish line.

And that finish line isn't just healing.

It's finally landing the career I've been working so hard for.

She's the one carrying me there.

She's been here since the beginning.

I passed the Final Exam and received my Digital Marketing Certification while under her roof.

If I wouldn't have been given the opportunity and freedom to dedicate my time to all I've learned over the past year, I wouldn't have had a solid career path I had already been building by the time I needed it more than anything in my entire life.

Sometimes I wonder if she realizes just how many lives she's changed.

I know Mike appreciated her.

I know I do.

Probably more than she'll ever fully know.

She has a way of quietly carrying people when they can't carry themselves anymore.

She doesn't do it because it's convenient.

She doesn't do it for recognition.

That's who she is.

She's the kind of friend that I feel like deserves her own chapter. Not just a section of someone else's.

Around that same time, my mom called and told me my dad's memory was getting worse.

Eventually came the MRI.

Eventually came the diagnosis.

Early-onset Alzheimer's disease.

I wanted so badly to be there.

I wanted to sit beside my parents.

I wanted to hold their hands.

I wanted to help.

I wanted to understand.

I wanted to walk through that chapter with my dad.

Life didn't unfold that way.

That hurt.

For a long time, I couldn't understand why.

Then February 2025 happened.

Looking back now, I think it became one of the greatest gifts of my life.

Without planning it…

Without even realizing it…

I spent almost the entire weekend with my dad.

It became just the two of us.

My mom was struggling with things of her own that weekend, so naturally, my dad and I gravitated toward one another.

And for the first time in years…

I felt like I had my dad back.

He was happy.

I was happy.

He kept finding reasons to knock on my bedroom door just to tell me something funny.

He told stories I'd heard my whole life.

I never once told him I'd already heard them.

I'd smile and listen like it was the first time because hearing him tell them was the part I loved.

Then he surprised me.

He told me a story I'd never heard before.

If no one had ever told me he had Alzheimer's, I honestly don't know if I would've known.

To me…

He was him.

The dad I'd known my whole life.

The person who always wanted to make sure everyone else was okay before himself.

The person who always made people laugh.

The person who made sure everyone was always treated equally.

He would make Honey a plate of hamburger each time he made my childhood dogs a plate.

She was just as important as they were.

My parents loved Honey because they knew how important she was to me.

In reciprocation, Honey loved them the second she met them.

Anyone who knows Honey knows how unusual that is.

Ever since I rescued her, she's been protective.

She usually barks at everyone she first meets.

Trust doesn't come easily to her.

You can just tell she has been through a lot by how attached she is to my every move.

How she depends on my reaction to make sense of things she may not fully understand at the time.

She's a fragile soul.

But when she met my parents…

She walked right into their house like she owned the place.

No barking.

No hesitation.

She walked right up to my dad as if she'd known him forever.

Watching the two of them together made my heart so full.

She followed him around.

He adored her.

She adored him.

Those simple little moments became memories I'll treasure forever.

About a month or so later…

I saw Mike for the last time.

By then, Honey had grown to love him too.

Unlike my dad, she was cautious at first.

She watched him.

She took her time deciding whether he was safe.

But once she trusted him…

She really trusted him.

I'd be in the living room, and she'd quietly leave my side just to climb up onto his bed and lay beside him.

He needed that.

And Honey knew he did.

Anyone who knows Honey knows how unusual that was to form a bond that quickly outside of my parents.

One thing is for certain, she chose him.

Just like she'd chosen my dad.

Looking back now, I can't help but smile at that.

My dad and Mike had things in common I began to realize as time went on.

Both incredibly funny.

Both unbelievably kind.

Both with hearts so gentle that even a timid rescue dog could feel it.

Sadly, both eventually diagnosed with diseases that slowly stole pieces of their memories.

But near the end, one thing is incredibly special that holds incredible value in how this all ties together.

Neither of them forgot who I was.

The fact I made that much of an impact…

I am so thankful.

My dad passed away on July 11, 2025.

Mike passed not long after.

Recent to writing this exact post.

Sometimes I sit and think about how close those final visits were.

The last time I hugged my dad.

The last time I hugged Mike.

I don't know why life unfolded the way it did.

I don't know why I wasn't there for every doctor's appointment.

I don't know why I wasn't able to walk every step of my dad's Alzheimer's journey beside him the way I wanted to.

I'll probably always wish I had.

But somewhere along the way…

Mike unknowingly gave me something I had been longing for.

Not another Dad.

No one could ever replace mine.

But through my friendship with Mike…

Through helping him…

Through laughing with him…

Through simply sitting beside him…

I experienced parts of a journey I desperately wanted to share with my own dad.

I learned the patience.

The tenderness.

The quiet moments.

The importance of simply being present.

Maybe that's why our paths crossed.

I can't say for certain.

None of us can.

But I know what it came to mean to me.

Looking back now, I realize this chapter isn't really about loss.

It's about gifts.

The gifts being the impact we make in each others lives.

My dad gave me one final weekend I got to spend at home, for just a little while, Alzheimer's disappeared.

He was my dad.

He was telling stories.

Making me laugh.

Keeping things clean and tidy any chance he'd get.

Checking on both my mom and I to make sure we were safe.

Checking to make sure I had enough gas.

Making sure my car was safe.

Making sure I had everything I needed to make life easier.

Just like he'd always done.

I wish I could call him and tell him about how I got a second interview as a Content Specialist at an Assisted Living facility.

All I wanted him to know was I was going to figure it out someday.

I don't get to do that in time.

That's why right now it's the most important thing on my list, that it's in fact the only thing on my list.

Tunnel Vision.

All day, Everyday.

I need my parents to know I can do this.

They deserve that.

What Mike gave me was a friendship I never expected but now can't imagine my life without.

He reminded me that some of the deepest friendships don't take decades to become family.

Sometimes they happen exactly when your heart needs them most.

My friend reminded me what unconditional love, trust and loyalty looks like.

She carried Mike when he needed someone.

She carried me when I needed someone.

She carried both of us at the worst times of our lives.

She gave both of us something every person deserves during life's hardest seasons.

A safe place.

And through all of them…

My dad.

Mike.

My friend.

Honey.

My Momma.

I finally saw the gift my family had been trying to tell me about all along.

Maybe my gift isn't something new I'll discover for the first time.

Maybe these very similar synchronicities were meant to show me the missing piece to the puzzle this entire time.

Landing my first job to kickstart my career.

Maybe marketing is simply the avenue through which I'll use it.

My real gift has always been people.

Seeing them.

Listening to them.

Making them feel like they matter.

Loving them through the chapters where they need someone to simply stay.

If that's true…

Then maybe my dad saw my purpose long before I ever did.

And maybe that's the greatest gift he ever gave me.
